# Business Partnership / Co-Ownership Discussion Notes

> **This one is deliberately a discussion checklist, not a
> ready-to-sign contract.** A real partnership/operating agreement
> depends heavily on your actual business entity type (LLC, general
> partnership, etc.) and state law — get a lawyer to draft or review the
> final document once you and your co-owner have agreed on the substance
> below. Signing something informal without that step is one of the
> most common ways small-business co-owners end up in a dispute later.
> See `legal/README.md`.

If Cooper River Trading Co. has more than one owner (e.g. the two
shared-Admin accounts referenced in the app's own auth setup), it's
worth having this conversation early, in writing, even informally at
first — most partnership disputes trace back to something the owners
never actually agreed on, not something that went wrong later.

## Ownership & contribution

- What percentage does each owner hold? **[e.g. 50/50, 60/40]**
- What did each owner contribute to start — cash, inventory, equipment,
  labor? Is that documented anywhere?
- If one owner contributes more time/work going forward, does that
  change ownership, get paid as compensation, or neither?

## Decision-making

- Which decisions need both owners' agreement (e.g. spending over $X,
  taking on debt, bringing in a new partner) vs. which can either owner
  make alone (e.g. day-to-day purchasing)?
- How are disagreements resolved if the owners are split 50/50?

## Money

- How and when do owners get paid — a salary, a profit split, or both?
- How is profit split if it doesn't match ownership percentage?
- Who has access to the business bank account / Stripe account, and how
  is spending tracked?

## If someone wants out

- What happens if one owner wants to leave? Does the other have the
  right (or obligation) to buy them out, and how would that be valued?
- What happens to shared assets (inventory, the Appraze account, the
  Apps Script/Google Sheet backend, domain names) if the partnership
  ends?
- Is there a non-compete or a "don't poach our suppliers/customers"
  expectation if someone leaves?

## What if one owner can't continue

- Disability, death, or simply stepping away — what's the plan? (This
  is exactly the kind of clause a lawyer will ask about and one that's
  easy to skip when things are going well.)

## Next step

Once you and your co-owner have real answers to the above, bring this
list to a lawyer to turn into an actual operating agreement (if you're
an LLC) or partnership agreement (if you're not yet formally an entity —
in which case, ask the lawyer whether forming an LLC first is worth it
for the liability protection). This conversation is worth having even
if the business is small right now; it's much easier before there's a
lot of money or hard feelings on the table.
