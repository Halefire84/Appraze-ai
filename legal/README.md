# Appraze / Cooper River Trading Co. — Legal & Business Document Library

A "have it ready before someone asks" library of the paperwork a small
resale business most commonly needs. These are **starting-point
templates, not legal advice** — see the disclaimer at the bottom before
using any of them for real.

App-facing legal pages (Terms of Service, Privacy Policy, Refund Policy
for Appraze itself) live in the app under **Legal** — see
`pages/9_📜_Legal.py`. Everything in this folder is different: back-office
paperwork for running the business day to day, not things a customer
reads.

## What's here and when to use it

| File | Use it when... |
|---|---|
| `NDA_Mutual.md` | You're discussing a deal, a supplier relationship, or business details with someone outside the company and want to keep it confidential before anything is signed. |
| `Independent_Contractor_Agreement.md` | You're paying someone (a photographer, lister, driver, VA) who isn't an employee — protects you both on scope, payment, and who owns the work. |
| `Consignment_Agreement.md` | Someone brings you items to sell on their behalf for a cut, rather than you buying the items outright (common for estate items, high-value pieces, or a seller who wants top dollar instead of a quick cash offer). |
| `Purchase_Agreement.md` | You're buying a lot, collection, or bulk inventory from an individual or business and want the sale (and who legally owned the goods beforehand) documented — not needed for routine small cash purchases, but valuable for anything large enough that a dispute would actually hurt. |
| `W9_Request_Letter.md` | You paid a contractor $600+ in a calendar year and need their taxpayer info for a 1099 at tax time. |
| `Business_Partnership_Notes.md` | You and a co-owner (e.g. the two of you running Cooper River Trading Co. together) haven't formally documented who owns what, who decides what, and what happens if one of you wants out. This one especially benefits from an actual lawyer — see below. |

## How to use one

1. Copy the template.
2. Fill in every `[bracketed placeholder]`.
3. Read it yourself, out loud if it helps — if a clause doesn't make
   sense for your situation, that's a sign to either fix it or ask
   someone who knows contracts, not to sign it anyway.
4. For anything with real money or an ongoing relationship at stake,
   have a lawyer review it before both sides sign. A one-time review is
   usually a flat, modest fee — cheap compared to a bad contract.

## When to actually hire a lawyer or accountant

You asked about this, so a straight, non-lawyer answer: there's no
universal dollar figure, but common signals small business owners use:

- **A lawyer**, once you're regularly signing contracts with people
  outside family/friends, once a single deal is large enough that a
  dispute would actually hurt, before formalizing a partnership/LLC
  structure with someone else (see `Business_Partnership_Notes.md`), or
  the first time someone else's lawyer sends you something to sign.
- **An accountant/CPA**, once bookkeeping stops being "a spreadsheet I
  update sometimes" — often when monthly revenue is consistent enough
  that you're spending real hours on taxes, once you've formed a
  registered business entity (LLC, etc.), before your first tax season
  as a business, or once you're deciding between reinvesting profit and
  paying yourself.

A one-time paid consultation with each (often a flat fee, not an
ongoing retainer) to sanity-check your setup is inexpensive insurance
relative to the cost of an avoidable mistake. You don't need either on
retainer from day one.

## Disclaimer

These templates are written to be accurate, standard, and genuinely
useful as a starting point — not filler. They are **not** a substitute
for advice from a lawyer or accountant licensed in your state, and no
one involved in writing them is your attorney or accountant. Every
`[bracketed placeholder]` needs your own real information, and every
template should be reviewed by a professional before you rely on it for
anything with real money or legal risk attached.
